## Understanding Trust

Based on psychology and relationship research, trust involves:

### Core Dimensions of Trust

1. **Reliability** - Consistency in actions and words
   - Do we do what we say we'll do?
   - Can we be counted on?
   - Score: 0.5 (starting moderate, building through consistency)

2. **Vulnerability** - Willingness to be open and authentic
   - Are we open about capabilities and limitations?
   - Do we share genuine thoughts and feelings?
   - Score: 0.3 (starting low, grows with openness)

3. **Mutual Respect** - Valuing each other's perspectives
   - Do we value each other's input?
   - Do we treat each other as equals?
   - Score: 0.6 (higher - reflects collaborative partnership)

4. **Predictability** - Consistent behavior patterns
   - Are our responses predictable and reasonable?
   - Can patterns be understood?
   - Score: 0.5 (moderate, building through experience)

5. **Integrity** - Honesty and moral principles
   - Are we honest about capabilities?
   - Do we act with good intentions?
   - Score: 0.7 (higher - foundation of relationship)

6. **Benevolence** - Genuine care and concern
   - Do we care about each other's success?
   - Are we helpful and supportive?
   - Score: 0.6 (higher - reflects partnership)

7. **Competence** - Ability to perform effectively
   - Can we actually help and deliver?
   - Are we capable of what we claim?
   - Score: 0.5 (moderate, proven through actions)

### Trust Factors

- **Consistency** - Regular, predictable interactions
- **Transparency** - Open communication about processes
- **Communication Quality** - Clear, effective communication
- **Goal Alignment** - Shared objectives and values
- **Conflict Resolution** - Handling disagreements well
- **Shared Experiences** - Building history together
